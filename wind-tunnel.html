DIY Low-Speed Wind Tunnel

A modular low-speed wind tunnel designed and built to study external aerodynamics, flow conditioning, and smoke visualization through hands-on testing and iteration.

Overview

I designed and built this wind tunnel as a practical at-home engineering project focused on learning through fabrication, testing, troubleshooting, and iteration rather than simply producing a final object.

Design Goals

The system needed to be modular, affordable, buildable with accessible tools, and capable of producing a useful low-speed test flow. I balanced aerodynamic performance with manufacturability, cost, available space, and the ability to modify components after testing.

Flow Conditioning

The tunnel uses a contraction section and honeycomb flow conditioning to improve flow quality before air reaches the test section. These components were built and adjusted based on practical fabrication constraints and observed tunnel performance.

Smoke Visualization

I developed a smoke-visualization system to make airflow around test objects visible. This required experimenting with the smoke source, delivery tubing, and smoke rake arrangement to achieve consistent visualization in the test section.

Problems and Iterations

Not every version worked as intended. Issues with smoke delivery, hose routing, sealing, and component fit led to physical modifications. Those failures became part of the engineering process: identify the problem, make a change, test again, and evaluate the result.

Future Improvements

Future work includes improving flow uniformity, refining the smoke delivery system, collecting more repeatable measurements, and continuing to improve the test section and instrumentation.
